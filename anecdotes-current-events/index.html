Anecdotes

1Station one

Stories about other people

An anecdote is a story about someone else that you've either observed or been told about. Like personal experience, it counts as first-hand evidence, and it's a strong way to appeal to pathos.

Personal experience

Something that happened to you.

"When I moved in eighth grade, I…"

Anecdote

Something that happened to someone else, that you saw or heard about.

"My neighbor moved here at fifteen without speaking English…"

A good anecdote gives an argument a human face. A fair one also represents others in the same situation, not just one unusual case.

Check yourself: what kind of evidence is it?

Santiago could have used facts and figures about the policy. Instead she focuses on one person, and is careful to show that Wendy's situation is typical of others the policy affects.

    Current events

    4Station four

    What's happening right now

    Current events are evidence you get first-hand through observation: by following what's happening locally, nationally, and globally.

    Why use them

    • Fresh, relevant, and familiar to readers
    • Show your argument matters now
    • Staying informed gives you a store of evidence for any essay

    Be careful

    • The same event can be interpreted many ways
    • Seek out multiple perspectives
    • Watch for bias in where you get the news
